PRE-LAUNCH LEGAL DRAFT · AUSTRALIAN LAUNCH CONFIGURATION

Terms of Service

This is a product governance draft for testing, not final legal advice or a representation that every law applies to every customer. A qualified Australian lawyer must review the final contracting entity, pricing, consumer terms, liability, privacy, employment/workforce features, complaint pathways and sector-specific obligations before commercial launch.

1. Service, trial and unpaid lifecycle

Super Pro AI Office Manager is an AI-assisted business operating workspace for service businesses. Test workspaces may receive a 14-day trial with an exact expiry date/time shown in the workspace. In the current product policy draft, an unpaid workspace may move to read-only restriction for 30 days after trial expiry, then to a retained archive period (default 183 days), followed by a final 14-day restoration window before deletion becomes due. The software does not automatically destroy customer data merely because a timer expires in this pre-launch build; production deletion must follow the published retention policy, lawful obligations, legal holds and authorised review. Billing is not activated unless an approved payment processor, final pricing, GST treatment and explicit payment authority are configured.

2. Account, business and contact verification

Users must provide accurate information and keep credentials secure. Australian workspace creation may verify an ABN, ACN or another supported business identifier and then verify the owner email and Australian mobile using one-time codes. Other identifiers may require manual or provider-supported review. Privileged users should enable multi-factor authentication. Attempts to bypass verification, tenant boundaries, permissions, rate limits or security controls are prohibited.

3. Organisation responsibility and user roles

The workspace owner controls authorised users, roles, permissions and business instructions. Owners must only grant access required for a person’s duties. Super Pro AI Office Manager may provide role templates for owners, managers, employees, workers and contractors, but the customer remains responsible for configuring lawful and appropriate access for its circumstances.

4. AI assistance and human governance

AI and automated features may draft, classify, summarise, route, recommend and prepare actions. Outputs can be incomplete or inaccurate. Legal, employment, disciplinary, safety, privacy, financial, complaint outcomes, payments and other consequential decisions require appropriate human review. Automated triage is not a final legal or factual determination.

5. Help, complaints and protected escalation

Users can access the Help & Complaint Desk to request support, make a complaint or report a security/privacy concern. Cases receive a reference and automated risk routing. Serious matters may be escalated to authorised senior users through configured channels. Confidential routing restricts access where configured, but selecting “confidential” does not itself create statutory privilege or whistleblower protection.

6. Whistleblower and workplace reports

Some Australian whistleblower protections apply only to eligible people, disclosures and recipients under applicable law. A general customer complaint or personal workplace grievance is not automatically a protected whistleblower disclosure. Customers must obtain advice and configure an appropriate recipient/policy where formal whistleblower obligations apply.

7. Personal information and workforce data

The customer must have an appropriate basis to collect, upload, use and disclose personal information. Sensitive or workforce information should be collected only when genuinely required, access should be limited by role and business need, and retention should be configured lawfully. See the Privacy Notice and Trust Center.

8. Communications, marketing and consent

Customers are responsible for lawful communications and marketing. Connection of email, SMS, social or messaging providers does not create consent to market. Promotional messaging workflows should retain consent evidence or another permitted basis where applicable, identify the sender accurately and respect opt-outs/suppression controls.

9. External platforms and integrations

Meta, WhatsApp, TikTok, YouTube, Snapchat, X, Google, telephony, email, payment, accounting and other integrations depend on third-party permissions, provider terms, APIs and service availability. App-level developer secrets should remain server-side. A provider is not represented as live until authorised credentials, permissions and a connection test succeed.

10. Security, audit and monitoring

The product includes organisation-scoped records, role-based access, authenticated sessions, rate limiting, optional MFA, security notifications, audit events and a tamper-evident governance ledger. No internet service can guarantee absolute security. Production launch also requires monitored infrastructure, secrets/key management, vulnerability management, backups, incident response, disaster recovery and independent security review.

11. Files, calls, recordings and evidence

Where enabled, the service may hold customer records, messages, call metadata/recordings, documents, media, work records and audit evidence. Customers are responsible for applicable notice/consent requirements for recording, monitoring and communications. Retention must be limited to legitimate business/legal needs.

12. Availability and changes

Pre-launch features, plans, limits, integrations and workflows may change. Provider outages or permissions can affect connected features. Material commercial terms will be published before paid production use.

13. Commercial launch items still requiring legal finalisation

Final contracting entity and contact information, governing law, consumer guarantees, cancellation/refund handling, payment terms, service levels, liability limits, indemnities, intellectual property, dispute process, data processing terms, subprocessors and sector-specific terms must be finalised before commercial release.
