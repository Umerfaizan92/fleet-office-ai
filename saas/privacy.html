PRE-LAUNCH PRIVACY DRAFT · AUSTRALIAN LAUNCH CONFIGURATION

Privacy Notice

This privacy notice is a product draft. Coverage under the Privacy Act 1988, APPs and other privacy laws depends on the entity and circumstances. Final data locations, processors/subprocessors, retention schedules, privacy contact and cross-border arrangements must be reviewed and published before commercial launch.

1. Information the service may hold

Depending on enabled modules: account and verification details; organisation settings; customer/enquiry records; quotes, bookings and jobs; staff/worker/contractor records; documents and qualifications; communications; call/SMS/email metadata and recordings where enabled; invoices/payments; social/content records; support complaints; AI task history; integration metadata; audit/security events and policy acknowledgements.

2. Purposes

Information may be used to verify accounts, provide the workspace, operate customer and workforce workflows, route communications, provide support, maintain security, record audit evidence, generate user-requested AI assistance, investigate incidents/complaints, meet lawful record obligations and improve service reliability. Personal information should not be repurposed incompatibly without an appropriate basis.

3. Transparent AI and automated workflows

Automated tools can classify, route, summarise and recommend. Consequential outcomes require human oversight. Where applicable, privacy policies and notices should explain significant automated decision arrangements. Australian APP 1 amendments concerning certain significant automated decisions commence on 10 December 2026.

4. Organisation isolation and access

Records are designed to be scoped by organisation. Server-side role checks restrict sensitive governance views. Owners/senior users may access organisation-wide audit, security and complaint administration where authorised; ordinary users receive role-relevant access. Confidential complaint routing can restrict access from a conflicted manager.

5. Security

Controls include verified registration, strong passwords, HTTP-only sessions, rate limits, optional MFA, role checks, provider secrets kept server-side, security alerts, append-only governance evidence and cryptographic hash chaining. Production infrastructure must add encryption-at-rest, managed key/secrets services, monitoring, backups, restoration testing and secure deployment practices.

6. Cloud and connected providers

A provider card in the interface does not mean data is being shared. Data should be sent to telephony, email, AI, social, storage, payment or accounting providers only after the integration is configured and authorised. Production must document provider purpose, data categories, locations, retention and cross-border/privacy implications.

7. Core storage architecture

Google Drive or OneDrive should not be used as the primary application database. Production should use an encrypted transactional database for structured data, encrypted object storage for calls/media/files, a secrets/key management service, and monitored backups/point-in-time recovery. Drive/OneDrive may be optional customer document integrations.

8. Retention, subscription restriction, deletion and de-identification

Retention should be purpose-based and category-specific. The current product policy draft uses a 30-day read-only recovery period after unpaid trial expiry, a retained archive period with a default of 183 days, and a final 14-day restoration window before deletion becomes due. These periods are configurable and must be reconciled with the final contract, lawful retention obligations, disputes, legal holds and privacy requirements before production. Personal information no longer required should be destroyed or de-identified where applicable unless lawful retention is required. Backup retention and deletion propagation must be included in the production schedule.

9. Access, correction, export and complaints

The production service should provide processes for authorised access, correction, export and deletion requests as applicable. Privacy concerns can be raised through the Help & Complaint Desk and routed to a designated privacy/complaints officer or authorised senior user.

10. Data breach response

Security incidents should be contained, investigated and assessed. Where the Australian Notifiable Data Breaches scheme applies, an eligible data breach may require notification to affected individuals and the OAIC. The software can assist evidence preservation and escalation but legal assessment remains a human responsibility.

11. Marketing

Marketing communications should maintain consent/permission evidence where required, sender identity, unsubscribe/suppression controls and separation of service communications from promotional content where appropriate.

12. Children and sector-specific information

The standard business workspace is not designed as a child-directed service. Health, legal, financial, education, disability, government and other regulated sectors may require additional privacy, security, retention and contractual controls before use.
